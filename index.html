Privacy Policy

The Chasms of Chaos · closed playtest, Android · Last updated 3 November 2026

In short

  • The first time you open the game it asks whether it may send usage data. If you tap Decline, nothing is sent and the game plays exactly the same.
  • If you tap Accept, the game sends a few events about how you use it, plus the details listed below, to Unity’s analytics service.
  • The playtest is for people aged 18 or over.
  • The game does not collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts.
  • You can ask me to delete your data.

1. Who I am

The Chasms of Chaos is made by Pratyush, a solo developer in India. I decide what data the game collects and why. You can reach me at chasmsofchaosdev@gmail.com.

2. Who the playtest is for

The playtest is for people aged 18 or over. Please do not take part if you are under 18. I do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18, and if I find out that I have, I will delete it.

3. What the game sends, and only if you accept

If you tap Accept, the game starts Unity’s analytics service and sends the information below. If you tap Decline, none of it is sent, and Unity’s analytics service is not started at all.

Events about how you play

  • When you open the game, and which version of the game it is.
  • When you start Training.
  • When you finish or skip Training, and how far you got.
  • When a match starts: whether the opponent is the computer or another person, which map, and the computer opponent’s difficulty.
  • When a match ends: whether you won, lost or drew, how long it lasted, the map, the difficulty and whether it ended by a win or by the clock.
  • When you save a deck. (Which cards are in it is not sent.)

What Unity’s tool adds

Unity’s analytics tool sends the following automatically with these events:

  • Your IP address.
  • A random install ID. The game creates it on your phone, and it is not linked to your name.
  • The platform (Android), the game’s version and the game’s app name.
  • About your phone: the model, the Android version, the language and region setting, the type of processor and graphics chip, the number of processor cores, the amount of memory, the screen size and resolution, the battery level and the volume setting.
  • When the game starts and stops, and how a session ended.

Unity can work out an approximate location, such as your country, from your IP address. The game does not use your GPS or ask for your location.

What the game does not collect

Your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, microphone or precise location. Your nickname is not part of the usage data.

4. Why I collect it

To see where testers get stuck (especially in Training), whether matches finish, and which phones the game runs on, so I can fix problems before a wider release. I do not use it for advertising and I do not sell it. The game has no ads.

5. Who else receives it

Unity (its analytics service stores and processes the data for me, and Unity’s own handling of it is covered by Unity’s privacy policy). Unity may process data in other countries. Photon receives some data when you play against another person (section 6). I do not share the data with anyone else.

6. Playing against another person

When you search for or play a match against another person, the game connects to Photon, a multiplayer service made by Exit Games. Photon handles your connection (including your IP address) and your nickname so the match can run, and the other player sees your nickname. This happens whether or not you accepted the usage-data notice, because an online match cannot run without it. Matches against the computer do not connect to Photon.

Your nickname starts as “Summoner” followed by four digits. If you change it, avoid your real name if you do not want the other player to see it. Photon’s own handling is covered by Photon’s privacy policy.

7. What stays on your phone

The game saves your decks, your nickname, your settings and your answer to the notice on your phone. Android’s backup feature may copy this saved data, including the random install ID, to your Google account and bring it back if you reinstall the game or move to a new phone. To start completely fresh, use your phone’s Settings > Apps > Chasms > Storage > Clear data (the names vary by phone).

8. How long it is kept

Unity keeps analytics data that can identify a device for 13 months by default (Unity’s documentation). Once I have finished analysing the playtest, I will ask Unity to delete the playtest data.

9. Your choices

  • Say no. Tap Decline when the game first asks. Nothing is sent.
  • Change your mind. The game has no setting for this yet. Use Clear data (section 7), open the game again and answer the notice again. Data that was already sent stays until it is deleted (next point).
  • See, correct or delete your data. Email me at chasmsofchaosdev@gmail.com. If you ask me to delete your data, I will look for your install ID in the analytics (it helps if you tell me roughly when you played and your phone model) and ask Unity to delete the data for it. The game does not show your install ID yet, so if I cannot tell which data is yours, I will tell you, and you can choose whether I delete all the playtest data instead.
  • Complain. Email me and I will reply within 30 days. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

10. Changes to this page

If I change this page, I will update the date at the top. Every change is recorded in this page’s public history on GitHub.
